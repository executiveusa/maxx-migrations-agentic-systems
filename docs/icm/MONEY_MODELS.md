# MACS Money Models

## Purpose

This is the commercial operating contract for MACS growth work. It turns broad marketing, product, automation, and advisory work into four client-facing outcomes only:

1. Reset
2. Momentum
3. Scale
4. Launch

No fifth public bucket is allowed without an explicit architecture decision.

The technology is not the headline. The business outcome is.

## The four buckets

### 1. Reset
Condition: value is leaking through a broken or scattered digital system.
Outcome: remove friction, recover trust, restore a clean path to action.
Money question: what is already costing the business leads, conversion, time, margin, or confidence?
Default first move: fix the highest-value leak before adding new channels or tools.

### 2. Momentum
Condition: the business has something worth selling but attention, follow-up, or consistency is weak.
Outcome: create a reliable flow of qualified attention and next actions.
Money question: what is the cheapest repeatable way to create more engaged prospects from what already exists?
Default first move: exploit warm reach, referrals, existing content, existing proof, and one clear lead magnet before adding complexity.

### 3. Scale
Condition: the idea is working.
Outcome: grow without complexity.
Money question: how do we increase output, revenue, or reach without requiring owner effort to rise at the same rate?
Default first move: systemize the working path, remove owner dependence, standardize delivery, then increase volume.

### 4. Launch
Condition: a new offer, company, product, program, or initiative needs proof in the market.
Outcome: get to real demand, first customers, and a repeatable acquisition path quickly.
Money question: what is the smallest credible launch that can prove willingness to act or pay?
Default first move: define the offer, audience, proof, CTA, and first measurable demand test before building the full machine.

## Shared value model

Every bucket must improve at least one of these and must not materially damage the others:

- desired business outcome
- confidence that the outcome will happen
- speed to first useful result
- effort required from the owner or customer

This is MACS' synthesis of the value-equation logic in *$100M Offers*: increase the desired outcome and confidence; reduce delay and effort.

## Lead model

Use the smallest working acquisition path first:

1. warm outreach
2. useful public content
3. cold outreach
4. paid advertising

Do not add a new channel while a current one can still be improved economically. Apply `more -> better -> new`: do more of what works, repair the constraint, then add a new placement/platform/channel only when marginal returns justify it.

Leverage paths are referrals, employees, agencies, and affiliates/partners. They multiply acquisition only after the core path is understandable enough to hand off.

## Offer model

Every offer must answer five questions in plain language:

1. Who is this for?
2. What valuable outcome changes?
3. Why should they believe us?
4. How quickly do they experience a useful win?
5. What do they have to do next?

Do not sell labor as the offer. Package a repeatable result and the process that produces it.

## Conversion model

Copy and calls must move a decision, not display expertise.

- One section, one job.
- One page, one primary action.
- Use concrete outcomes before mechanisms.
- Name the owner/customer condition before naming our capability.
- Prefer a direct question or next step over a generic `Learn more`.
- Do not use AI, agentic, automation, model, orchestration, or stack language unless the mechanism itself is necessary to the buyer's decision.

Default MACS CTA: `Tell us what's important.`

## Story model

Stories are proof and movement, not decoration.

Use this sequence:

1. Current tension: what was stuck, costly, confusing, or at risk?
2. Decision: what changed in the way the problem was framed?
3. Work: only the parts of the process that matter to the outcome.
4. Evidence: what can be shown or measured?
5. Future: what became possible next?

The listener/customer remains the center of the story. Use real details. Never invent metrics, testimonials, relationships, or outcomes.

## System model

A good result is not enough if it depends on one heroic person.

- standardize the repeatable part
- document it so another capable person can execute it
- quantify the inputs and outputs
- remove recurring owner bottlenecks
- preserve human judgment for truth, priorities, relationships, approvals, and exceptions

The target is a business that can keep producing the result without constant founder intervention.

## The commercial scorecard

Every experiment must record:

- bucket
- target customer
- problem
- offer
- channel
- CTA
- spend
- owner time
- leads
- engaged leads
- calls / qualified opportunities
- sales
- revenue
- gross profit when known
- time to first useful result
- evidence link
- decision: kill / keep / improve / scale

No vanity metric can overrule cash, qualified pipeline, conversion, retention, owner time, or demonstrated customer value.

## Lowest-hanging-fruit rule

Prioritize work using this MACS synthesis:

`Priority = (expected commercial impact x confidence x speed) / (cash cost x owner effort x complexity)`

This is a prioritization heuristic, not a financial forecast. Use real data as soon as it exists.

## Source lineage

This model synthesizes principles from:

- Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*: value equation, offer differentiation, problem/solution construction, speed and effort reduction, proof and pricing logic.
- Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*: engaged leads, lead magnets, Core Four, More-Better-New, lead getters, measurement.
- John Warrillow, *Built to Sell*: specialization, standard service offering, recurring demand, predictable sales, reduced owner dependence.
- Michael E. Gerber, *The E-Myth Revisited*: work on the business, systemization, innovation/quantification/orchestration, predictable results.
- Phil M. Jones, *Exactly What to Say*: decision-focused language, future scenarios, precise questions, simple next steps.
- Les Brown, *7 Principles of Powerful Storytelling*: listener journey, tension to possibility, future orientation, feedback and revision.
- Marty Neumeier, *Brand A-Z*: differentiation, customer journey, customer vision, touchpoints, brand as a coordinated system.
- `blader/humanizer`: remove formulaic AI-writing patterns, filler, false contrasts, inflated language, and unsupported claims.
- `virgiliojr94/book-to-skill`: extract reusable frameworks, principles, techniques, anti-patterns, and voice calibration instead of creating book summaries.

## Guardrails

- Facts before claims.
- Proof before promises.
- Real scarcity only.
- Real urgency only.
- No fabricated guarantees.
- No invented metrics.
- No dark-pattern persuasion.
- Human approval for pricing, guarantees, public claims, spend, contracts, publishing, and consequential outreach.
