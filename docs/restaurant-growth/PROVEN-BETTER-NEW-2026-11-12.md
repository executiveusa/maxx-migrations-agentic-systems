# PROVEN–BETTER–NEW CARD — MACS Restaurant Revenue + Creator Operations
Mode: Professional | Research as of 2026-11-12 | Status: **RESEARCH / NOT A FEATURE-PARITY CLAIM**

**Instinct:** A time-poor restaurant owner wants more profitable customers while trusting that someone competent handles the repetitive marketing work and can show what was actually earned.

**Primary analog:** [Feast Analytics / Remy](https://feastalytics.com/hire-remy) — marketed directly to independent restaurant owners, overlapping MACS's target buyer. The brand's own product screenshot, pricing and restaurant-specific case studies support audience match, but not independently verified business outcomes. Secondary analog: [Owner.com](https://www.owner.com/pricing) for already-installed restaurant ordering/loyalty, and [Thanx](https://www.thanx.com/) for retention/holdout-style measurement.

## PROVEN — considerations (original functions, never visual copies)
1. **Acquisition / first run — Remy:** Local recruitment campaigns bring creators who apply directly to one restaurant. Can MACS obtain owner-authorized applicant source and a clear restaurant-specific benefit without buying access to someone else's creator network? Smallest test: one approved public creator-application link with a consented real applicant.
2. **Core loop — Remy:** Applicant → decision → booking → reminders → verified visit → uploaded content → approval → optional ad usage. Are all transitions persisted and reconciled, rather than displayed as demo cards? Smallest test: one consented applicant and rejection/approval rollback in disposable tenant.
3. **Retention / recurring supply — Remy:** Repeated creator recruitment and automated after-approval follow-up appear to be the platform's repeat-use engine. Can Popebot reduce actual human messages without unauthorized texts or invented fulfillment? Measure real operator touches per content delivery.
4. **Pricing — Remy:** Public $300/month proposition with roughly $150 software and suggested $150 recruitment media; creators may also consume restaurant-provided meal credit. A no-extra-subscription MACS solution still incurs media, food, texting, hosting, human review and maintenance costs. Will restaurant contribution margin cover them?
5. **Distribution / growth — Feast:** Full system produces local creatives and paid campaigns rather than relying on organic post impressions. Will owner-approved existing channels/ordering outperform buying new ads for Bunna? One channel, one attributed paid-order experiment.
6. **Compounding data — Feast/Owner:** Stored candidate histories, successful content formats, POS/loyalty data and owner-approved briefs may compound value. Are rights, ownership, deletion and export preserved across clients? Do not build a new shadow CRM.

## BETTER — considerations
1. **Cash-inclusive proof:** Feast publicly describes Toast/Square/Clover POS matching; do we distinguish recorded cash receipts from unreliable campaign guesses, with source receipt reconciliation? Test one real anonymized cash POS entry.
2. **Contribution profit instead of ROAS:** Feast's public examples report attributed gross revenue; can MACS use food costs, delivery/processing, creator meals, staff time and fee to explain an actual *contribution* decision? Test one owner-confirmed meal margin and fully costed group order.
3. **Denominator honesty:** Feast's own [Remy 2026 benchmark](https://feastalytics.com/blog/restaurant-influencer-marketing-benchmarks-2026) says 329 delivered-content visits from 2,635 applications as of its cutoff, with incomplete future/pending records. Can we make applied/booked/checked-in/content-approved/ad-run separately visible, without inflated conversion rates?
4. **Operator effort:** Published benchmark reports a median 25 tracked messages around a completed visit; can Popebot prepare permitted follow-up, flag failed deliveries and reduce review burden while preserving opt-out and consent?
5. **Owner sovereignty:** Owner.com demonstrates that a restaurant may already pay for ordering/loyalty/agent functions. Can MACS remain a lightweight read-only overlay and export tool, rather than requiring costly POS replacement?
6. **Adjacent variant:** A local workplace group-meal pipeline with creator coverage as *support*, not a volume influencer farm. Could that deliver higher incremental contribution per campaign than individual discount coupons? Test one approved group order.

## NEW — considerations
1. **Popebot as supervised creator coordinator:** Cross-system permissions for recruitment, scheduling, reminders, content and ad metadata are unproven. Quarantine to one manually controlled application→approval→audit receipt first.
2. **API-callable multi-POS broker:** Square read-only prototype is coded; Toast/Clover/Owner runtime permission and normalized transactions are unknown. Do not advertise integrations before official sandbox/owner OAuth proof.
3. **Causal small-restaurant impact engine:** Genuine incrementality is hard to estimate at small sales volume. Test a holdout or daypart comparison with pre-declared minimum sample/uncertainty, never invent lift.
4. **Automatic creator rights tracking:** Asset usage rights, third-party releases, paid-ad whitelisting and expiry need human/legal verification. Test a single explicitly signed/approved license before any campaign.
5. **No subscription for client:** MACS's internal tooling can avoid a second vendor fee but is not free to operate; who bears human media capture, recruitment ads, creator meals, SMS, payment platform fees, support and hosting? Price the first experiment in real costs.
6. **Improvement loop:** Automated offer selection/scaling should not be a core assumption until actual attributed + incremental contribution is measured. Keep automation read-only and decision-support until approved.

**Graveyard / failure mechanism:** Deep-discount coupon marketing can be margin-negative, as noted in historical studies of daily-deal restaurant use (e.g. Rice University's 2011 business survey); this is not evidence that Remy/Feast failed. A creator pipeline that ends in views rather than tracked purchases can also be economically inconclusive. No independently verified identical product failure was established.

**Anti-signal:** Prior $1,000 spend, enthusiastic team reaction and a pretty mockup are not customer-order revenue or feature adoption. Only real transactions, durable operational receipts and owner-verified outcomes count.

**Sharpest tensions:**
1. A no-subscription internal system still has unavoidable recurring operating costs. Who pays when campaign contribution profit is low?
2. Feature completeness needs staff authorization, real provider consent and durable state; screenshots alone cannot provide the creator network or operational reliability.
3. Building everything before the first order spends the margin we are trying to generate. Which one workflow proves a profitable paid customer first?

This is an evidence and tension map, not a verdict. Next: one observed, consented receipt/creator operation before broadening to a full production feature set.
