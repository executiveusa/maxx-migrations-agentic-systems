# OFFER.md — MACS Custom Offer & Interactive Flipbook Protocol

> **Status:** Agent instruction / proposed reusable workflow, not proof of a deployed offer generator.
> **Owner:** MACS Digital Media. **Canonical authority:** MAXX Migrations ICM and commercial policies.
> **Classification:** USE / MERGE with the existing MAXX growth engine. Do not create another control plane.
> **Mission:** Scan one authorized prospective business, identify its actual business constraint, propose the smallest credible MACS intervention, and produce an original, client-specific interactive proposal modeled on the *mechanics* of the Crown & Core flipbook.
> **Default operating mode:** Private preview and human review only. Do not send, publish, invoice, sign, deploy publicly, or start a client agent without explicit authorization.

## 0. How an agent invokes this protocol

Given a business name, a public website URL, or an existing authorized client workspace, perform the entire safe sequence **DISCOVER → EVIDENCE → DIAGNOSE → FIT → OFFER → STORYBOARD → BUILD → TEST → REVIEW**.

Example owner instruction:

> Read OFFER.md, the current MACS policies, and the Crown & Core reference. Research [business name / URL] using public and authorized sources. Build a custom private pre-audit and 90-day proposal flipbook with a recommended MACS path, a truthful value stack, and an optional HyperFrames introduction. Preserve all approval gates. Return the verified private preview, evidence ledger, recommended offer, and questions that actually require an owner decision.

If only a name is supplied, establish the correct business identity before making claims. Never silently substitute a similarly named business. If a site is unavailable, use verified public materials and label missing evidence. Do not ask routine questions until accessible sources are exhausted.

**Completion contract:** One working, mobile-readable, interactive private flipbook and an inspectable research/offer packet—or a precise BLOCKED/PARTIAL report saying what could not be verified. A static mockup or PDF alone is not completion.

## 2. Business scan — observe, don't speculate

**Inputs:** business name/URL, geographical/service scope when known, business category, prospective decision maker if supplied, allowed sources/permissions, optional client interview notes, and target problem if already known.

**Public/authorized scan checklist:**

- Business identity: canonical name, address/service region if relevant, official domain, active social profiles; verify identity across sources.
- Website: mobile usability, load/navigation, home page clarity, calls to action, service/product/menu information, contact methods, broken journeys, accessibility, SEO fundamentals and trust signals.
- Customer journey: discover → understand → enquire/order/book → confirmation → follow-up; execute only harmless test navigation unless explicitly authorized to submit synthetic requests.
- Local discovery: Google Business Profile, public directory accuracy, reviews and owner replies, where available. Attribute and date every review claim; never infer revenue or conversion from star ratings.
- Social/content: active channels, recency, audience-fit, owned assets, content gaps, link-to-action path and evidence of consistency.
- Competitive context: at most three genuinely comparable local peers when that improves a business decision; avoid broad vanity-score comparisons.
- Operations: publicly visible gaps; treat missed calls, poor follow-up, owner overload, CRM weakness, booking losses or manual admin as **hypotheses** until the owner supplies evidence.
- Existing assets: site, booking, email, CRM, content library, analytics, ad accounts, customer lists **only when explicitly authorized and appropriately scoped**.
- Business economics: ask for actual baseline lead volume, response time, conversion rate, average order/customer value, owner hours and gross margin only if needed. Unknown is an acceptable value.

**Acceptable tools:** Firecrawl for public page text/map, browser/Playwright for visual journey inspection, public search and business listings, read-only authorized connector access. Respect robots/terms, rate limits, privacy and platform rules. No credential harvesting, paywall bypass, security exploitation, unapproved outreach or real transactions.

Write an evidence ledger: ID, observation, exact source URL or authorized source ID, observation date/time, screenshot/receipt path where relevant, evidence type (**VERIFIED / DOCUMENTED / INFERRED / UNKNOWN**), confidence, and owner confirmation required.

Never invent screenshots, ratings, customer quotes, campaign performance, bookings, medical claims, pricing, client consent or quantitative ROI. Preserve contrary evidence and unknowns.

## 3. Diagnose the single money-facing constraint

Use the canonical four-bucket definitions; **do not invent a fifth public bucket**:

| Path | When to lead with it | Working promise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reset** | Valuable demand is leaking through broken, confusing, scattered or untrusted systems | Simplify what got complicated. |
| **Momentum** | An offer exists, but attention, follow-up, content or qualified demand is inconsistent | Stay visible. Build in public. |
| **Scale** | The idea works; capacity, owner dependence or operational complexity constrains growth | The idea is working. Grow without complexity. |
| **Launch** | A new company, offer, service, program or product needs proof of demand | Bring your next idea or project to market. |

Choose the **primary path from the observed business condition**, not from the size of the contract MACS would prefer. If more than one path fits, lead with what is blocking money closest to an existing customer today. State alternative secondary paths in one sentence.

The **three engagement levels** can be presented as Reset, Momentum and Scale; **Launch remains a fourth route/starting circumstance**, not a suppressed fourth MACS program or a new price tier. For a Launch prospect, create a Launch-specific scope sized to one of the approved engagement levels and mark the mapping in the internal offer rationale.

Produce:
- The real customer pain in everyday language (one sentence).
- Observed evidence supporting it (at least two distinct signals where available).
- What remains a hypothesis pending interview.
- The smallest useful win in 7–30 days, if realistic.
- One primary 90-day outcome when the selected engagement warrants 90 days.
- The single best metric and its baseline/status.
- What would make the offer a poor fit.
- One owner-facing next action.

Follow the value hierarchy: cash already owed → existing lead recovery → inbound response → conversion/follow-up → qualified demand → only then broader scale. Do not propose an extensive autonomous infrastructure project for a simple website problem.

## 4. Package the custom offer — never force all three tiers

Use **MACS MAXX SUITE** as a managed partnership, without making tools the headline. The prospect sees what happens for their business, while MACS manages technical execution.

The internal packaging candidates, **not pre-approved public price quotations**, are:

| Level | Example planning anchor | Default scope direction |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| **MAXX Reset** | USD 1,950 one-time | Audit plus one bounded, measurable digital repair and handoff. |
| **MAXX Momentum** | USD 4,500 for a scoped 90-day partnership | Smart site or meaningful upgrade + inquiry/follow-up path + bounded approved content and learning loop. |
| **MAXX Scale** | From USD 17,500 for a scoped 90-day engagement | Integrate up to a few prioritized proven workflows, expand human-approved operations/creative delivery, and measure results. |

These are **historical/internal planning anchors, not authority to quote**. Confirm approved price, number of deliverables, expenses, payment schedule, optional retainer, service limits, tax/currency, data ownership, refund/remediation terms and signature language with the MACS owner. Do not silently carry any Crown & Core contract terms into another proposal.

Offer design requirements:
1. Lead with **one recommended level**, its fit, one measured business constraint and an understandable first win.
2. Show the other levels only when useful for comparison; they must not overwhelm the buyer.
3. Explain exactly what MACS builds, what stays managed, what the owner approves, and what the client keeps.
4. Provide a 30/60/90-day schedule **only for a genuine 90-day engagement**; shorter Reset scopes need a truthful shorter timeline.
5. Separate setup/onboarding, 90-day labor, optional ongoing service, and billable consumption (hosting, SMS, AI API, telephony, ads, media).
6. State explicit exclusions: guaranteed leads/sales/rankings, ad spend, unlimited content, autonomous quotations/payments, unverified booking or voice, and unsupported integrations.
7. Create a fair **working-deliverable guarantee draft** based on objective acceptance tests and a bounded fix period, not speculative ROI. No guarantee is binding or publishable without approval.
8. Apply the value-equation discipline: important outcome, higher confidence, faster first useful result, lower owner effort. Do not inflate promises with fictitious dollar values.
9. Fit the owner's words and circumstances. Use a discovery → demonstration → decision structure from the Jasper smart-site research, never a canned stack pitch.
10. Keep the owner's ownership of domains, source, data, credentials and portable records explicit. Clarify rights to MACS licensed product code and third-party software separately.

Never promise that Instinct, Paperclip, Hermes, automatic content publishing, CRM updates, voice calling or payments are live for this tenant without direct environment-specific evidence. The owner's interface may be **Agent MAXX**, with Instinct an optional supported front door, and Hermes/Paperclip behind the scenes, subject to real integration and approval gates.

## 5. Flipbook editorial structure

Create a **short, premium, prospect-specific pre-audit / proposed partnership**, never a generic 36-page clone. Start with approximately 10–16 meaningful pages, expand only when real evidence and approved contract requirements justify it.

Suggested chapter flow:
1. **Cover:** prospect name, original brand treatment where authorized, "Prepared for [business]" and MACS credit.
2. **Personal note:** why MACS looked at this business; no invented relationship.
3. **What we saw:** 2–4 factual, evidence-linked observations.
4. **The customer's journey:** where friction may occur; visibly distinguish observed steps from hypotheses.
5. **The opportunity:** one important improvement rather than 12 services.
6. **The recommendation:** primary Reset / Momentum / Scale / Launch fit.
7. **What we will do:** a short, concrete value stack matched to the business.
8. **First visible win:** how MACS demonstrates useful progress.
9. **Delivery timeline:** phased and true to the scope.
10. **How the partnership works:** owner approvals, human MACS responsibility, assistants where actually supported.
11. **What stays yours:** assets, portability, access, documentation and limits on third-party licensing.
12. **Investment / alternatives:** only owner-approved exact price, scope, term and exclusions; otherwise label PRIVATE PRICE REVIEW and do not show prospects.
13. **Evidence and limitations:** sources, unknowns, assumptions, and verification status.
14. **Decision:** one low-friction next action, default "Tell us what's important."
15. **Back cover:** original prospect-appropriate treatment and MACS credit.

Use a human voice, editorial visual hierarchy, and reduced cognitive load. No buzzword-first copy; no "AI army," "revolutionary orchestration," or vague "10× growth" claims. The client should understand the proposal without knowing the software stack.

## 6. Flipbook build — match mechanics, not client copy

**Inspect the current Crown & Core repo rather than relying on an old description.** As of this protocol draft the relevant files are index.html, book.js, styles.css and phone-reader.html; the documented current reader uses dependency-free DOM/CSS, desktop 3D sheet turning and native horizontal scroll-snap on small screens. Older React/react-pageflip revisions exist in prior work but are **not the current baseline**.

Reusable behavior to preserve where compatible:
- Genuine 3D desktop book with hard front/back cover and believable page shadow/curvature.
- **Mobile-first portrait reader** with native scrolling; do not force a tiny desktop spread onto phones.
- Accessible Contents navigation, previous/next, keyboard arrows, page number/progress, deep-linked chapters, and readable live/selectable text.
- Optional page-turn sound **off by default**; obey reduced-motion preferences and avoid intrusive autoplay.
- Fullscreen only if it adds genuine value; work without it.
- Reading-position restore, print-friendly output, offline HTML or matching PDF **as secondary formats**.
- Dense-page overflow protection; never clip content, headings, prices, disclaimers, sources or CTA.
- Real client-appropriate brand styling with MACS' editorial standards. Do not copy Crown & Core's logo, protected client copy, leather aesthetic or specific imagery automatically.
- One verified private preview link that opens, navigates, reads on mobile, and can be reviewed in one click.

Avoid a generic dashboard-card aesthetic. The book should feel like a thoughtful custom proposal, not a screenshot of a pitch deck.

**Do not mutate or deploy the Crown & Core reference repository.** Reuse reviewed reader code in a separately owned implementation only when licensing/rights permit; otherwise adapt the design patterns independently. Preserve the original client experience.

## 7. HyperFrames motion — supportive, not a dependency

Use the existing executiveusa/pauli-hyparframe-video patterns and upstream HyperFrames (heygen-com/hyperframes) **only after verifying the installed tooling**. HyperFrames creates deterministic HTML-native video via browser rendering and FFmpeg; it is not the 3D page-flip engine.

Optional single client-specific 6–12-second composition:

- Frame 2: simple visualization of the specific issue/opportunity, labeled honestly.
- Frame 3: the recommended direction and MACS partnership mark.
- Original typography and properly licensed/customer-approved assets.
- Silent by default, captions for meaningful speech, still-poster fallback, and reduced-motion alternative.

Render/encode only if credits, software, licensing and compute are already authorized. Validate a real MP4 and responsive playback before claiming the motion intro exists. Otherwise deliver a working static-cover flipbook and mark HyperFrames as PENDING. Motion must never block the offer or degrade reading accessibility.

## 8. Agent roles, Paperclip and ICM

Use existing Hermes/Paperclip integration **only when a supported connection is actually verified**. Do not create another agent orchestrator, database, scheduler or queue.

A reusable task graph with one accountable owner:

1. **Researcher (read-only):** identity, sources, screenshot/evidence ledger.
2. **Offer strategist:** four-bucket routing, main pain, value stack, fit and draft pricing questions.
3. **Editor/art director:** concise client-specific story, logo permissions, editorial design.
4. **Builder:** isolated flipbook implementation and optional separate HyperFrames composition.
5. **Verifier (independent):** evidence audit, accessibility/mobile, functional and commercial claims.
6. **Human reviewer:** approves public/client communication, final price, guarantee, permissions and actual offer.

Hermes coordinates; Paperclip may track assignments and outcomes; ICM remains the business truth and approval ledger. Workers never self-approve. Use versioned task receipts, stable client/workspace IDs, task retries with limits, and tenant isolation. The existing Agent MAXX portal remains an authorized fallback if Instinct cannot be connected.

If Paperclip is unavailable, perform the same tasks sequentially with the existing tools and accurate task status. Never claim synthetic agents are running.

Store no passwords, OAuth tokens, private client PII, sensitive business reports or raw CRM exports in a public repository or public preview. Use private authenticated preview or client-approved access. Strip metadata that leaks secrets.

## 10. Build/review gates

**Gate A — Scan:** Business identity and core cited evidence checked. Unknowns visible. No unsupported accusations or inaccurate third-party claims.

**Gate B — Offer:** ONE primary MACS route and ONE recommended engagement, with smallest useful win, measurable metric and nontechnical explanation. Internal prices only until approved.

**Gate C — Copy/rights:** No copied client text, unlicensed media, invented proof, unapproved quotes/guarantees or leaked confidential data.

**Gate D — Working reader:** Test the book in a real browser at 320, 360, 375, 390, 414, 430, 768, 1024 and 1440 CSS pixels; verify page turns, portrait reading, Contents, touch/keyboard, progress, print/offline behavior as applicable and zero content clipping. Run reduced-motion and keyboard-only tests.

**Gate E — Technical reliability:** Run available lint/build/typecheck/smoke tests, direct preview navigation, links/CTAs, 404, privacy and exposure checks. Inspect resulting artifact, not only unit-test logs.

**Gate F — Motion:** Separate HyperFrames preview/render QA if supplied; missing motion is not missing book functionality.

**Gate G — Independent review:** Another reviewer inspects evidence, pricing state, copy, mobile screenshots, and claims. Builder cannot sign off its own release.

**Gate H — External release:** MACS owner authorizes the exact prospect, exact artifacts, exact price/terms, delivery channel and date. Publish/send only after explicit approval; no automatic sending, paid spend, public case studies, signing link or client onboarding.

**Rollback:** Keep original reference repos unchanged; preserve previous private preview and source SHA; disable/withdraw new preview independently. Never weaken security to make a demo work.

## 11. Completion report and prospect handoff

Report with:

- **Decision:** principal Reset / Momentum / Scale / Launch path and why.
- **Business scan:** concise findings with source IDs and date.
- **Offer:** recommended level, real deliverables, term, approved/unapproved price status, exclusions, expected first win.
- **Proof:** preview URL actually opened; viewport/interaction tests; source SHA; independent reviewer result; HyperFrames video verification separately.
- **Status:** READY FOR OWNER REVIEW / PARTIAL / BLOCKED, never "sent" or "live" without evidence.
- **Commercial value:** immediate business problem and realistic 30-day path to discussing or closing a paid engagement, without inventing likelihood.
- **Risk/rollback:** unverified claims, missing permissions, integrations or source details; safe recovery procedure.
- **Next action:** one decision the owner must make to authorize delivery.

**Final operating law:** Research their real business, make the value obvious, recommend only what fits, build a beautiful usable book, prove the mechanics work, and keep the owner in control. The purpose is a credible **conversation and paid customer outcome**, not another agency showcase or internal agent framework.
