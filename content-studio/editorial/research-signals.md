# Editorial Research Signals — 2026-08-10

These are topic-discovery signals, **not prevalence statistics**. A Reddit post can show that a problem is concrete and worth investigating; it cannot prove how common the problem is.

- Reddit Shopify communities, 2026: recurring store-review threads call out weak homepage structure, missing trust signals, poor mobile spacing, overcrowded navigation and unclear above-the-fold messages.

  - Editorial use: Shopify readiness, trust checklist and mobile-first review topics.

- Reddit / r/dropshipping, May 2026: “Why your Shopify store looks scammy in 2026 (even if it isn't).”
  - Signal: first-impression trust and generic template patterns are active concerns.
  - Editorial use: storefront trust review; do not repeat unsupported conversion claims from the thread.

## AI moving beyond chat

- Reddit / r/n8n, July 2026: “Building an AI second brain/ADHD assistant, which tool to use as foundation?”
  - Signal: users explicitly want systems that capture, organize, connect to calendars/tools and act proactively rather than another standalone chatbot.
  - Editorial use: explain chatbot vs agent vs automation, context, permissions and practical business jobs.

## Editorial rule

Before any full article is approved:
1. verify current product facts with primary vendor documentation;
2. distinguish anecdotal community signals from measured evidence;
3. add an original MACS framework, checklist, process lesson or founder commentary;
4. remove any claim that cannot be supported;
5. keep the reader's next step understandable without technical expertise.
