# MACS Boring Revenue Systems

## Core doctrine

Prefer boring recurring problems over impressive technology.

The best candidate is usually a job a business already pays a person to do, forgets to do, does slowly, or does inconsistently.

Do not begin with an AI capability. Begin with a commercial leak or repetitive job.

Ask:

1. What repetitive task is already costing or making this business money?
2. What happens when it is late, missed, inconsistent, or never done?
3. Can we connect into the tools the business already uses?
4. Can we demonstrate the result before asking for a large commitment?
5. Can the first win expand naturally into a larger operating system?

## Route by bucket, not technology

The same automation can fit different buckets depending on the business condition.

### Reset
Use when money or time is already leaking.

Typical wedges:
- overdue invoice follow-up
- missed-call capture / receptionist
- lead response delay repair
- old lead reactivation
- inbox triage when founder attention is the bottleneck
- broken follow-up workflows

Primary proof:
- recovered cash
- fewer missed opportunities
- faster response time
- owner/team hours saved

### Momentum
Use when the offer works but demand or follow-up is inconsistent.

Typical wedges:
- content repurposing
- personalized outbound
- website lead capture and qualification
- referral / warm follow-up systems
- nurture sequences

Primary proof:
- engaged leads
- qualified opportunities
- booked calls
- conversion rate

### Scale
Use when the path already works but cannot grow cleanly.

Typical wedges:
- receptionist / intake automation
- lead routing
- end-to-end follow-up
- multi-channel lead engine
- standardized prompt/playbook hub
- owner-dependence reduction

Primary proof:
- output per owner hour
- throughput
- conversion retained as volume rises
- reduced manual handling
- gross profit contribution

### Launch
Use when a new offer needs fast market proof.

Typical wedges:
- one outbound campaign to a narrow ICP
- one lead-capture surface
- one speed-to-lead sequence
- one follow-up path
- one demoable automation tied to the offer

Primary proof:
- first qualified conversations
- first booked calls
- first sale
- time to first useful demand signal

## The offer ladder

Do not sell the full machine first unless the client already has evidence and urgency.

### Level 1 — Wedge
A small problem with visible value and low implementation friction.

Examples:
- invoice follow-up
- inbox triage
- missed-call text / callback
- content repurposing

Goal: trust + first measurable win.

### Level 2 — Money-facing system
Tie the automation directly to acquisition, conversion, cash recovery, or retention.

Examples:
- personalized outbound
- website lead qualification
- speed-to-lead
- lead reactivation
- AI receptionist

Goal: prove a repeatable commercial effect.

### Level 3 — Integrated revenue engine
Combine only the components that have already demonstrated value.

Possible sequence:
1. capture
2. qualify
3. respond fast
4. book
5. reactivate
6. follow up
7. collect
8. report

Goal: predictable growth without owner effort scaling linearly.

## Full reduction pass

For every candidate automation, reduce it to:

- Problem: one sentence.
- Money consequence: one sentence.
- Smallest useful system: one sentence.
- Evidence metric: one line.
- CTA: one line.

Bad:
`We build an AI-enabled omnichannel autonomous agentic workflow stack.`

Good:
`You are paying for leads that nobody calls back fast enough. We call and qualify every new lead within minutes and hand the interested ones to your team.`

## Full call pass

The next action should feel smaller than the problem.

Preferred calls:
- `Show us where leads are getting lost.`
- `Send us the last 30 days of missed calls.`
- `Show us the overdue invoice list.`
- `Show us what happens after a new lead comes in.`
- default MACS CTA: `Tell us what's important.`

Do not ask the buyer to understand architecture before they can proceed.

## Collins-level storytelling pass

Tell the business story in this order:

1. the ordinary recurring failure
2. what that failure costs
3. the smallest intervention
4. the evidence
5. what becomes possible next

Keep the technology in the background unless it materially changes trust, risk, or buying criteria.

## Pricing logic

Price from business value and risk, not build hours alone.

Inputs to collect before recommending price:
- current lead volume
- current conversion rate
- average customer value / gross profit when known
- number/value of missed calls or unworked leads
- unpaid invoice value when relevant
- manual hours currently spent
- implementation risk
- ongoing optimization burden

Valid structures:
- implementation fee
- monthly maintenance / optimization
- per qualified appointment or performance component when attribution is clean and explicitly approved
- hybrid setup + retainer

Never invent ROI. Build the value case from the client's own baseline.

## Human-in-the-loop rule

Default to human approval when the action can create material reputational, financial, legal, or customer harm.

Examples:
- draft before send for sensitive email
- alert founder instead of auto-reply for urgent matters
- review content before publish
- verify appointment data before committing when confidence is low
- require approval before bulk outbound

Automation should remove repetitive work, not remove judgment where judgment matters.

## Demo-before-complexity rule

Prefer a working narrow demo over a large proposal deck.

A good demo lets the buyer see one real business event move through the system:

`input -> decision -> action -> recorded outcome`

Examples:
- overdue invoice -> follow-up -> status update
- new lead -> callback -> qualification -> booking
- missed call -> answer -> appointment
- old lead list -> conversation -> booked call

## Experiment rule

Every offer starts as an experiment until real evidence says otherwise.

Record:
- bucket
- wedge
- target ICP
- baseline
- value hypothesis
- implementation cost
- owner/team time
- leads / opportunities / sales / cash recovered
- time saved
- failure modes
- customer feedback
- keep / improve / kill / scale decision

Use the standard experiment template in `../experiments/TEMPLATE.md`.

## Lowest-hanging-fruit ranking

Score candidates by:

`(commercial impact x confidence x speed) / (cash cost x owner effort x complexity)`

Prefer:
1. existing demand over new demand
2. existing leads over buying new leads
3. existing unpaid cash over speculative pipeline
4. existing workflow integration over replacement projects
5. one repeatable job over a broad transformation

## Anti-patterns

Reject:
- AI for AI's sake
- selling features before identifying a commercial problem
- automating high-risk judgment without an approval gate
- buying more traffic while current leads are leaking
- replacing working systems unnecessarily
- giant multi-agent builds before proving one wedge
- vanity metrics used as proof of commercial success
- performance pricing when attribution is ambiguous
- bulk outreach without explicit approval and compliance review

## Exit condition

A boring automation graduates into a productized MACS system only when:

- the problem recurs
- the buyer can understand the value in one sentence
- the system produces observable evidence
- delivery can be standardized
- maintenance burden is understood
- the next expansion step is obvious

Then package it under the appropriate public bucket. Do not create a fifth bucket.
