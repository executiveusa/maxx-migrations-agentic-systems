# Launch

## Public outcome

**Put the idea in front of the right people. Learn what earns a yes.**

Launch is for a new offer, product, company, program, initiative, or market entry that needs real proof before more complexity is added.

## Money condition

Start here when:

- the offer is new or materially changed
- the audience is not yet proven
- the business has no reliable acquisition path for this thing
- the team is tempted to overbuild before anyone acts
- positioning is still based mostly on assumptions
- first customers, first revenue, or first qualified demand are the critical unknowns

## Full reduction

Write five lines only:

1. **Audience:** who is this for?
2. **Problem:** what do they already want changed?
3. **Outcome:** what becomes better for them?
4. **Proof:** what can we truthfully show today?
5. **Call:** what single action tests demand?

## Launch sequence

1. State the target customer and urgent problem.
2. Define one clear offer.
3. Reduce the promise to the outcome, not the mechanism.
4. Assemble the minimum credible proof available.
5. Build the minimum launch surface needed to understand and act.
6. Give it one primary CTA.
7. Start with warm demand when available.
8. Record objections, engaged leads, qualified opportunities, sales, revenue, and owner time.
9. Improve the offer or message from evidence.
10. Add channels, features, or infrastructure only after the current test answers its question.

## Minimum credible launch

A launch does not automatically require:

- a full platform
- a large website
- every integration
- every feature
- a large paid campaign
- a long brand manifesto

It requires enough truth, clarity, proof, and delivery capability to test whether the right people will take the next meaningful action.

## Story pattern

Use future-oriented storytelling without pretending the future has already happened:

- the current condition
- the possibility
- why this approach exists
- what can be shown now
- the invitation to act

Never turn an aspiration into a result claim.

## Primary metrics

- time to first engaged lead
- qualified opportunity rate
- time to first sale
- first revenue / gross profit
- offer conversion rate
- show rate / close rate when calls are involved
- customer acquisition cost when paid acquisition is used
- objection frequency
- activation / first-value completion when the product can be used

## Stop conditions

Launch has done its job when the team has enough real market evidence to choose among:

- **Reset** if the path or product is broken
- **Momentum** if demand exists but needs consistency
- **Scale** if the idea works and capacity/repeatability is now the constraint
- **Kill / rethink** if the evidence does not support continued investment
