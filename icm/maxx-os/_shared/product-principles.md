# MAXX Product Principles

These are durable product laws for MACS, Agent MAXX, MAXX Migrations, and software built from this system.

## 1. Complexity underneath. Simplicity on top.

The implementation may be sophisticated. The owner's experience should get simpler.

## 2. Understandability before capability.

Do not lead with everything the system can do. Make it obvious what is happening, what happens next, and what the human needs to decide.

## 3. Reduction before addition.

Before adding a screen, feature, workflow, field, sentence, dependency, or agent, ask whether something can be removed, combined, automated, or made unnecessary.

## 4. One obvious path.

Every important surface should have one clear job and a clear next action.

## 5. Meet people where they already are.

Prefer voice, text, email, familiar messaging, and existing workflows over new dashboards when they are safe and sufficient.

## 6. Technology stays backstage.

Agents, models, APIs, automation, databases, and orchestration are infrastructure. Do not turn them into the user's burden.

## 7. Quiet competence.

The system should feel capable without constantly announcing that it is AI-powered.

## 8. Human control remains real.

Automation reduces clerical work; it does not remove owner authority over money, truth, relationships, credentials, public claims, destructive actions, or acceptance.

## 9. Preserve before rebuilding.

Inspect first. Reuse what works. Prefer the smallest intervention that creates a verified outcome.

## 10. Proof before claims.

Built is not verified. A green CI run is not production proof. Evidence must match the claim.

## 11. Owner sovereignty.

Code, domain, data, credentials, workflows, context, analytics, and exports remain practically controllable by the owner.

## 12. The reduction test.

Before shipping, ask:

**Can anything else be removed without reducing clarity, trust, utility, safety, character, or owner control?**
